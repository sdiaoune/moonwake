# Moonwake: The Long Dawn campaign

The player cleared the original game in about ten minutes and asked for a longer, richer journey, with **more exploration**. The expansion therefore has 24 substantial stages across eight worlds. Each stage comprises three continuous authored rooms; reaching a room exit continues the same stage, clock, stars, and discoveries without an intermediate clear menu. The 72 rooms contain 128,792 world pixels, approximately 6.46 times the original campaign's traversal space. Individual stages span 5,152–5,560 pixels, roughly three times the original individual stages.

A 60–90 minute first journey is the design target. It is not a claimed human measurement. Experienced clears can remain faster. Length comes from authored routes, new environments, discoveries, and encounters rather than forced waiting, reduced movement speed, grinding, or collectible gates.

## Exploration comes first

Every new room presents a fluid main route, at least two substantial scenic alternatives, and a third discovery route or tucked-back pocket. The 60 new rooms contain 172 annotated forks with at least four platforms and 280 pixels of actual route space. Many stretch across several screens. They have entry and rejoin points, star or lantern clues, encounter choices, and recoverable lower landings. Optional loops ask the player to turn back briefly toward a rear roof alcove or find a lower passage under a bridge. The original twelve first rooms remain recognizable and geometrically unchanged.

Three late rooms connect previously separate scenic forks into optional roof networks. Jellydew Hollows links the library return into its loop; Memory Margins threads a returning roof alcove through two lit bridges into its moving balcony; Last Lantern Walk crosses from a ridge into the spring crown. Four new one-way bridges and seven hovering star/lantern clues preserve the broad catches and every existing seal. Their junction annotations describe both directions and stable returns.

The three seals in each room are discoveries, not tolls. All nine stage seals are optional; none gates a room, boss, world, or ending. Lower routes and roof routes are equally valid ways to enjoy the scenery. A player can ignore a high challenge, leave a pocket unfinished, and return later through the atlas. The stage rank records the current run rather than turning saved discoveries into automatic medals.

High bridges over lower alcoves use one-way platforms so their foreground does not hide the passage below. Grotto routes descend to y128, follow a lower star trail, and return through a y104 stepping ledge. Roof loops travel forward over an upper gallery, then turn back onto a visible rear roof alcove. Moving balconies have stable launch and catch surfaces nearby; lantern routes have visible charge anchors. These are distinct route experiences, not three identical tiny roof ladders.

## The journey

Kip's scarf carries a little warmth through a sleeping sky sea. The original harbor, rain garden, moonworks, and whale horizon become the first half of a longer journey. The sky's lost wishes then appear as festival lights, observatory charts, aurora fruit, and booklights in a dawn archive. Guardians are confused dream knots to loosen, rather than inhabitants to conquer. Four world finales culminate in the Rainbell Warden, Comet Manta, Prism Sentinel, and finally the Dreamwhale.

## Progressive movement and encounters

Kip retains the original acceleration, variable jump, and quick horizontal scarf dash. Difficulty grows through combinations and choices rather than shortening every landing. New ground phrases use rises of 16–32 pixels, wide useful rest shelves, and readable forward jumps. Three late rooms teach spring arcs, three include compact moving ledges with catch floors, and two teach long lantern transfers in the main phrase; the remaining moving ledges belong to scenic branches. Players see optional moving festival floats over broad ground before meeting those crossings.

There are 24 moving platforms. Their base left edge oscillates horizontally by ±16 pixels over 128 active ticks; phase follows platform index. Width is 24–40 pixels, with visible cream tops and stable launch/catch surfaces nearby. Grounded riders move with the platform. The route validator includes both endpoint offsets when assessing gaps.

Twelve rooms have gentle wind. It affects only airborne movement, does not alter vertical physics, and stops during a scarf dash. The first rightward current occurs on the broad Foxfire Promenade. Later counterwind appears on the Comet Almanac and Windfall Lanes. Every wind room's intro explicitly says `WIND CARRIES RIGHT.` or `WIND CARRIES LEFT.`; room transitions must expose that line through a brief active-play banner, because direction changes should be visible even when there is no intermediate menu.

The aurora and archive worlds contain 53 jelly encounters. Jellies patrol horizontally with a small ±8-pixel vertical drift. They sit above broad boughs or chosen upper routes, with stable lower alternatives; they do not crowd spring or moving launch edges. Earlier worlds use beetles and owls spaced over broad surfaces. Crumbling leaves and gear teeth remain optional in newly authored rooms. The original v1 spring, crumble, and 96-pixel dash lessons are preserved in their first rooms.

Every room has a checkpoint. Ordinary checkpoints lie near the middle of a stable main landing, away from the edge. Advancing to a new room also establishes that room's start as a checkpoint. Boss rooms instead put their checkpoint 248 pixels before the goal, close to the fight; all seals precede the combat apron. Each combat apron has 496 pixels of continuous solid y128 floor and no ordinary enemies. A boss retry does not replay half the room.

## Route data and native interfaces

`docs/routes.json` is the canonical source. Its top-level `levels` list contains 24 stages; each has three complete `rooms`, a full-stage par, total stage length, and boss type. Room-zero fields also remain on the outer stage for older consumers. Every room records all native platforms, pickups, and enemies, along with its ground phrase, discovery paths, movement lesson, encounter notes, and landmarks.

`main_route` is the complete authored path, including a moving ledge when present. In new rooms, `safe_platform_indices` lists the stable main surfaces; it is a subset rather than a replacement for `main_route`. Each `seal_routes` entry specifies `entry_main_platform`, ordered `platform_indices`, `rejoin_main_platform`, `seal_pickup_index`, and `seal_platform_index`. Lower paths may collect the seal before the final return step. Controller tests must collect when reaching `seal_platform_index`, rather than assuming every seal is on the branch's final platform. Backward route segments are intentional in annotated loops and pockets.

`LevelDef` keeps the existing field order and appends `mechanic`. Constants are `LEVEL_COUNT=24`, `WORLD_COUNT=8`, and `ROOMS_PER_STAGE=3`, with unchanged 48-platform, 64-pickup, and 12-enemy limits. `PLATFORM_MOVER=4` and `ENEMY_JELLY=3` are the only new object kinds.

Bank 3 contains the dispatcher. World campaign data lives in banks 16–23, nine rooms per bank. `level_load_room(stage, room, ...)` calls the correct `BANKED campaign_N_load`, which copies its own constant arrays into caller-owned RAM before returning. No ROM pointers escape and no C function switches away from its executing bank. `level_load(stage, ...)` remains a room-zero wrapper; `level_info(stage, ...)` returns room-zero information. `level_intro_room` and `level_intro` fill three caller-owned buffers of at least 21 bytes. `level_par(stage)` returns the full three-room par in seconds.

All room lengths stay between 1536 and 1856 pixels, below the signed fixed-point coordinate limit. The largest authored room has 26 platforms, 45 pickups, and eight enemies, leaving native headroom. Full-stage S pars range from 95 to 120 seconds. They derive from independently observed all-nine clear ticks multiplied by 1.45 and rounded up to five seconds. They are optional replay tuning values, not evidence of human completion time and not a gate on progress.

## Reproducibility and quality gates

Run `python tools/build_campaign.py` after editing the canonical routes. It writes the dispatcher and eight campaign source files. Run `python tools/build_campaign.py --check` to verify exact generated sources and `python tools/check_campaign.py` for static validation. `--json` on the validator provides detailed controller-risk annotations.

Validation covers 24 stages, 72 rooms, 216 seals, native caps and coordinates, useful checkpoints, boss positions, continuous combat floors, wind signposting, mover bounds and catch surfaces, discovery clues, substantial forks, complete seal paths, and unique new ground compositions. Stored release signatures prove that the original twelve first-room names, dimensions, spawn/goal/checkpoint positions, platforms, pickups, enemies, and main paths match v1.0.0. Boss placement is deliberately changed only for the expanded structure.

Static validation passes, and the dispatcher plus all eight campaign files compile with GBDK without content warnings. The final accepted all-seal controller recording completes all 24 stages, 72 rooms, 216 discoveries, and four bosses with zero deaths. A separate blank-SRAM linear replay reproduces the ending and all permanent seal masks; reboot restores completion and all 24 S medals. Its 108,466 video frames are about 30 minutes of expert controller traversal, not a measured first-player journey. `dist/playtest-report.json`, `dist/verified-inputs.json`, `dist/rank-reboot-report.json`, and `dist/capture-manifest.json` bind this evidence to ROM SHA `6ef653eba34dae18385501266ee266b689d857823db28ca5fd2c3d304f6a0721` and 72 fresh native room captures.

A separate 80,248-frame main-lane recording completes every authored main surface and four bosses with zero deaths. Its fresh linear replay records actual native contact with every annotated main platform in `dist/main-route-report.json`. The three linked roof networks also pass controller-earned forward and reverse traversal, followed by fresh blank-SRAM linear replays. `dist/junction-report.json` records native contact with all four new bridges in both directions, activation of all seven new star/lantern clues, and collection of an existing discovery beyond each link. No proof depends on gameplay-memory edits, and earlier-ROM recordings remain private historical artifacts.

Integration must verify all-room controller traversal, fresh linear replay, all-seal collection, room transitions, checkpoints, cross-room discoveries, save/resume, migration, and boss gates. Independent critics should specifically audit exploration depth, whether clues actually appear on screen, repeated branch patterns, hidden lower passages, spring recovery, dynamic catches, and whether discoveries feel optional. The 60–90 minute target should be judged through human play; no timer padding or fabricated score should substitute for that evidence.
